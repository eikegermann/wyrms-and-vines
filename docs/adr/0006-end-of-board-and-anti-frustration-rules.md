# 6. End-of-board tension and anti-frustration rules

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** November 2026 (recorded 2026-11-22)

## Context

Playtesting with three players showed that the endgame needs tension, so a leader can still be caught. The start needs a little flavour, and some layouts felt mean rather than exciting.

## Decision

- **Top row:** no vine reaches it; vines end on 90 or lower.
- **End zone:** the last two rows (81–99) always have 2–4 wyrm heads, with at least one on 95–99. One of the two guaranteed end wyrms is mild (a 10–16 drop) and the other a proper ouch (22–35), placed in random order.
- **A way up:** a small-to-medium vine (11–24) always starts in the first row, and at least one vine ends on 80–85.
- **First row:** at most one wyrm ends there.
- **No double punishment:** after a drop of more than 20, there's no wyrm head within 6 squares (one die roll) of the landing square.
- **Wyrm spacing:** two wyrm heads must be at least 2 + floor((dropA + dropB − 5) / 5) squares apart, and never fewer than 2. Meaner pairs sit further apart.

## Consequences

Games stay open until the end, and the frustrating combinations don't happen. The spacing formula is our current reading of the idea, with "space" taken as the difference between head squares. It may be refined after more playtesting.
